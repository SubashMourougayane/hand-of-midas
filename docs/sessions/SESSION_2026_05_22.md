## Key Learnings

### From the Eval Debate (FAILURES.md)
- 55 failed strategies tested before finding winners
- Phantom fills can inflate results by 89% — always verify fills are at achievable prices
- EMA-lag trailing stops produce impossible exits after crashes
- "Institutional-grade" claims need OOS validation, not just IS numbers
- Walk-forward Fold 4 showed decay — mean-reversion struggles in crash regimes

### Architecture Decisions
- **No trailing stops anywhere** — fixed SL/TP/time exits only
- **Shared fill_model.py** — backtest and live use identical logic by construction
- **DB is truth** — everything persisted, nothing in memory-only dicts
- **Separate backends per instrument** — no logic conflicts, independent schedulers
- **One order per trade** — place with SL+TP, OANDA manages, we monitor
- **Price stream for break-even** — not polling (60s was too slow)
- **Full ISO timestamps** — not just dates (journey chart needs exact bar time)

### Numbers to Remember
- Gold: 1,012 trades, 59.7% WR, PF 3.40, $177k on $5k/yr
- Oil: 390 trades, 72.3% WR, PF 8.31, $315k on $5k/yr
- Combined: $492k over 20 years, zero losing years
- Tiered risk: Alpha 4%, Mean-Rev 3%, Cross 2%
- Max DD: Gold -19%, Oil -14.9%
- Trading frequency: ~68 trades/year combined (~1.3/week)

### What NOT to Do (learned the hard way)
- Don't use trailing stops (phantom fills)
- Don't store just DATE for trade timestamps (journey chart breaks)
- Don't use `=== "alpha_sweep"` (misses `"alpha_sweep_oil"`)
- Don't hardcode API_BASE in frontend components
- Don't mark ALL backtest runs as not-latest (cross-contaminates instruments)
- Don't compare equity ($5000+) against mean of individual PnLs ($50)
- Don't check `sl >= entry` for SHORT break-even (always true for shorts)
- Don't mix GBP P&L with USD equity tracking
- Don't show post-trade bars in journey chart (misleads about what happened)
