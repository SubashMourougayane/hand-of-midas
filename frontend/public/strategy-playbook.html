What Is a Liquidity Sweep Reversal?

The Core Idea (in plain English)

When price moves sideways for a while, traders place their stop-loss orders just above and below that range. Big players know this. They push price past the range to trigger those stops (collecting "liquidity"), then immediately reverse direction.

We wait for that fake-out move, confirm it with a strong reversal candle, and trade in the opposite direction — riding price back into (and through) the range.

Three things must happen in order:

1. Consolidation (price moving sideways in a range for several hours)

2. Sweep (price spikes past the range to grab stop losses, then snaps back)

3. Engulfing (a big reversal candle that completely wraps the previous one — our entry signal)

SHORT Trade — Visual Breakdown

Price goes sideways, spikes UP (fake breakout), then a big red candle says "that was a trap." We sell.

SHORT trade: Sweep above the range triggers stop losses, then price reverses down. We sell on the engulfing candle.

LONG Trade — Visual Breakdown

Same pattern but flipped. Price goes sideways, spikes DOWN (fake breakdown), then a big green candle says "trap over." We buy.

LONG trade: Sweep below the range triggers stop losses, then price reverses up. We buy on the engulfing candle.

The Bias Filter — Yesterday Decides Today

One Simple Rule That Adds 10% Win Rate

Look at yesterday's daily candle. If it was a big red day (sellers dominated), we only take SHORT trades today. If it was a big green day (buyers dominated), we only take LONG trades today. If it was small and indecisive, we take trades in either direction.

Why? Momentum tends to continue. Don't fight a strong trend just because you see a pattern.

The bias filter is the simplest rule in the system, but it prevents the most painful trades.

The 4 Systems — Same Strategy, Different Settings

All 4 systems use the exact same liquidity sweep reversal strategy. The difference is how they find the range and how long they wait for the setup.

Why 4 systems instead of 1?

Micro (rolling windows every 2 hours) catches more frequent, smaller moves throughout the day.

Macro (fixed Asia session as the range) catches the big London/New York opening moves.

Running both Gold AND Oil gives uncorrelated trades — when one instrument is quiet, the other often moves.
